Aim & Objectives

Man-in-the-Middle Attack on Diffie–Hellman

Aim

To demonstrate a Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attack on the Diffie–Hellman key exchange algorithm by illustrating public-value interception, substitution, and the establishment of separate shared secrets.

AliceEveBob
Objectives
  1. Understand the principles of Diffie–Hellman key agreement.
  2. Demonstrate interception and substitution of exchanged public values.
  3. Compare normal communication with a MITM-compromised exchange.
  4. Observe how separate shared secrets and derived session keys affect message confidentiality and integrity.
  5. Identify authentication methods that prevent public-value substitution.

Theory

Diffie–Hellman lets two parties agree on a shared secret over an insecure network. Only public values travel; private exponents never leave their owners. A KDF then turns the secret into a session key.

Why does the attack work?

Unauthenticated Diffie–Hellman never verifies who supplied a public value. An active attacker can replace values in transit and keep a separately keyed connection with each side.

ConfidentialityMessages may be exposed IntegrityMessages may be altered AvailabilityAffected if traffic is blocked

Prevention: authenticated key exchange — verified digital signatures, certificates, or an authenticated pre-shared secret.

Experimental Steps

Follow the complete virtual experiment in three phases.

A

Setup

  1. Enter the public prime modulus p and generator g.
  2. Enter Alice's private exponent, Bob's private exponent, and Eve's single private exponent.
  3. Calculate Alice's and Bob's public values.
B

Key exchange

  1. Run the normal exchange to observe the matching Diffie–Hellman shared secret.
  2. Switch to MITM mode and observe Eve's interception of public values.
  3. Observe Eve's substitution of both public values with Eve's public value.
  4. Calculate the two separately established shared secrets.
  5. Observe how matching or separate session-key material can be derived from those secrets.
C

Message & analysis

  1. Send Alice's original message and observe its delivery without interception.
  2. In MITM mode, observe Eve's interception, modification, and forwarding of the message.
  3. Compare Alice's original message with Bob's received message.
  4. Analyze the attack's security implications and authentication-based prevention.
